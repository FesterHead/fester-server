🔍 How Winner Selection & Cryptographic Verification Works

Every Maintenance Challenge uses a commit-reveal cryptographic protocol and public randomness so that winner selection is 100% transparent, immutable, and mathematically verifiable by any participant—with zero possibility of playing favorites or moving the goalposts.

1. The "Sealed Envelope" (Pre-Committed Strategy Hash)

Before a maintenance challenge even opens, the administrator configures the winning rule for that day (for example: random or a secret nth finisher like 5th place) along with a secret random salt.

The Sealed Envelope Analogy:
Imagine writing "The 5th person to finish wins — Secret Code #8891" on a card, sealing it inside a tamper-proof envelope, and taping it to the front of the room before anyone starts solving the puzzle. Nobody can see through the envelope while playing, so everyone tries their best—but the host also cannot swap out the card after seeing who finished 5th!

In digital form, that sealed envelope is the Strategy Commitment (SHA-256) hash displayed at the top of the leaderboard from minute one: 

strategy_commitment = SHA256("<secret_salt>:day=<YYYY-MM-DD>:strategy=<strategy>")
# Or for the N-th finisher strategy:
strategy_commitment = SHA256("<secret_salt>:day=<YYYY-MM-DD>:strategy=nth:n=<N>")
  • Why you can't guess it beforehand: Because <secret_salt> is a random UUID kept on the server until the winner is minted, players cannot brute-force the SHA-256 hash to peek at whether the day's rule is random or nth.
  • Why the admin cannot change it afterward: SHA-256 is a one-way cryptographic hash function. Changing even a single character (such as changing n=5 to n=3 or switching nth to random) completely scrambles the resulting 64-character hex hash. When the winner is minted, the full unhashed string (the Commitment Preimage) is published on the leaderboard so anyone can hash it and confirm it matches the exact strategy_commitment that was visible all day.

2. The Winner Selection Strategies

Each maintenance challenge day uses one of two pre-committed strategies. Once the administrator runs the minting command, the winner is permanently locked in the database (INSERT OR IGNORE) and cannot be re-run or altered.

🎲 Strategy A: Provably Fair Random (random)

When a challenge states a random winner will be chosen from everyone who finishes, how do you know the admin didn't just click "pick random winner" ten times until their favorite person popped up?

Instead of using a private random number generator, the minting script fetches the latest public pulse from the League of Entropy (drand)—an independent, distributed randomness beacon operated by Cloudflare, EPFL, Protocol Labs, and global universities that publishes a verifiable cryptographic signature every 30 seconds.

  1. The script fetches the current drand_round and drand_randomness (which anyone can verify directly at https://api.drand.sh/public/<round>).
  2. It combines the revealed commitment_preimage, the drand beacon values, and the ordered list of all finishers (1:alice,2:bob,...) into a single string and hashes it with SHA-256 to produce the Selection Hash.
  3. The winning rank is determined purely by modulo arithmetic on that 256-bit hash: 
    winning_rank = (int(selection_hash, 16) % total_completions) + 1

😈 Strategy B: Secret N-th Finisher (nth)

Before the challenge begins, a specific finisher position N (for example, n=5 for the 5th person to finish) is locked inside the pre-committed SHA-256 hash: 

commitment_preimage = "<secret_salt>:day=<YYYY-MM-DD>:strategy=nth:n=5"

What if fewer than N people complete the challenge? The selection formula uses modular wrap-around across the total number of finishers so there is always a valid winner without human intervention: 

winning_rank = ((N - 1) % total_completions) + 1

Because n=5 is baked into the Commitment Preimage, revealing the winner proves beyond a shadow of a doubt that N was chosen before the challenge started—not after looking at the leaderboard!

3. Verify It Yourself in Your Terminal

Don't trust this web page either? You can verify both the Strategy Commitment and the Winning Rank from any Linux, macOS, or WSL terminal using standard sha256sum and python3: 

# 1. Verify that the revealed Commitment Preimage matches the Strategy Commitment hash:
echo -n "PASTE_COMMITMENT_PREIMAGE_HERE" | sha256sum

# 2. For a 'random' draw, verify the Selection Hash and Winning Rank in Python:
python3 -c '
import hashlib
selection_input = "PASTE_COMMITMENT_PREIMAGE|drand_round=ROUND|drand_randomness=RAND|participants=1:user1,2:user2"
h = hashlib.sha256(selection_input.encode()).hexdigest()
total = 8
print("Selection Hash:", h)
print("Winning Rank  : #", (int(h, 16) % total) + 1)
'
